Philosophy

We negotiate the beginning of the next deal

A transaction closes in weeks. A programme holds for years. Both get signed the same way; only one builds anything.

No agreement is designed to maximise the first sale. It is designed to make the client want to come back, which is more demanding: it requires a price that funds the service, a margin that works for them, and the real capacity to deliver in a hard year.

We wrote this way of thinking into a book. It is not marketing material: it is the standard decisions get made against in here.

Our philosophy

A company that uses wine to build businesses

Corbeau makes wine. What decides how it gets made is not oenological: it is economic.

Before settling a blend we ask what price it has to hit on the shelf, what it competes against, what margin the importer needs, and what has to be true for that product to still exist in five years.

An estate winery starts with the wine it wants to make. We start with the business that has to be built.

Our history

Third generation. The same ground

The Rodríguez family has made wine in Mendoza for three generations. The winery sits in eastern Mendoza and works fruit from its own vineyards, spread across different zones, soils and altitudes.

That spread is not picturesque. It is a tool: it lets us compose the same profile from different origins when a year turns difficult in one zone and we still have to deliver.

Three generations do not make us better. They make us findable: everyone knows where to find us next year.

Leadership

Who answers when something goes wrong

In a family company the person who decides is the person who picks up the phone. There are no three approval layers between your problem and whoever can solve it.

That has a cost: we cannot hide behind a procedure. And an advantage: we do not try to.

Culture

What we do not do

It is easier to describe a company by what it refuses.

We do not promise lead times we cannot meet in order to win an order. We do not quietly adjust quality when the harvest comes in badly: we say so beforehand, even when it costs the order. We do not accept a price that will not fund the service that price promised.

Every one of those decisions cost us specific business. That is the price of a word that still means something.

Certifications

What an outside auditor can verify

Trust begins in conduct and ends in a document somebody outside can check.

Our commitment

Sustainable means it is still there later

We look after water, soil and by-products because a vineyard is a fifty-year asset and gets managed as one.

The part almost nobody names is the economic one. A supplier without margin stops investing, stops controlling and ends up failing to deliver. A programme that only works by squeezing the weakest link has an expiry date.

A sustainable business is one where every party has reasons to continue.

Terroir

Several origins for one profile

An estate wine expresses a place.

An international programme needs the opposite: a profile that holds even when the place changes.

Vineyards across different zones, soils and altitudes let us offset a weak year in one with what another delivered, and ship the same bottle.

Vineyard management

It is decided at pruning, not in the winery

The profile is settled before harvest: crop load per vine, irrigation, picking date.

We work backwards from the commercial objective. If the programme needs fruit, the vineyard is managed for that. If it needs structure, it is managed differently.

The agronomic conversation starts with a price.

Winemaking process

The objective is not the best wine. It is the same wine

Any winemaker makes an extraordinary wine once. Making it eight years running, across eight different harvests, is another problem entirely.

Yeasts, temperatures, maceration and final blend are all subordinated to a profile documented with the client.

Nothing is improvised, upwards or downwards.

Thermovinification

The tool that rescues the difficult year

It applies controlled heat to the fruit before fermentation. It extracts colour and fruit far more efficiently than traditional maceration and reduces the dependence on a perfect harvest.

It solves the most expensive problem an international programme has: vintage variation. It reaches the colour and intensity committed to in a year that would not have delivered them on its own.

It is technology, not magic. And it is one reason we can commit to a profile across several years.

Quality control

What happens when something goes wrong

It will go wrong at some point. Over more than forty years of operation, it goes wrong.

What defines a supplier is not the absence of problems: it is what they do in the hour that follows.

You are told the moment it is found. How fast the warning comes is what defines a supplier, not the absence of problems.

First it gets fixed. The lot, not the conversation.

Then the process gets corrected. So that particular error is no longer available.

The client hears it from us. And before the problem reaches them.

Backwards from the price

We start with the number, not with the wine

A private label project has a shelf price, a channel margin, a logistics cost, a tax and a tariff. What is left is the wine that can be made.

First we close the economics. Then we design the product that fits inside them.

The other way round, you end up with an excellent sample nobody can sell.

Starting point

The price your wine has to reach on the shelf

  • −

    Retailer margin

    It changes with the channel: a supermarket chain does not ask for the same as a wine shop or an online store.

  • −

    Importer or distributor margin

    It depends how many steps sit between the winery and the shelf. Sometimes one, sometimes two.

  • −

    Domestic taxes in the market

    Every country has its own, and they do not levy alike: some by value, others by volume or by alcohol content.

  • −

    Import tariff

    It depends on the trade agreement in force with Argentina in that destination.

  • −

    Logistics, insurance and clearance

    It varies with the format, the distance, the time of year and the exchange rate.

  • −

    Packaging: bottle, label, closure and case

    This is the part the project can move on purpose. Lightening a bottle frees cents that go back into the wine.

Validation

The approved profile is documented: analytical parameters, composition, sensory description. That document is the technical contract for everything that follows.

It is what lets someone reproduce the same wine four years from now, with another harvest.

Production

We produce against the specification and verify every lot against the approved sample before it leaves.
