The best wine is the one that gets bought again

Not the most awarded. The one that occupies a precise place on a shelf, at a price that works, and rotates.

Designing that wine is a commercial problem before it is an oenological one.

The starting point

Three facts, and the rest follows

Shelf price. Market. What it competes against.

Everything else comes out of that: the available cost per litre, the fruit that cost allows, the process, the format, the bottle weight, the closure. Every technical decision is a consequence of an economic constraint.

A project that starts with «we want a premium Malbec» has not started yet.

Shelf price · market · competition

The only three facts needed to begin.

Available cost per litre

What is left after margins, taxes, tariff and logistics.

The fruit that cost allows

Zone, variety, yield and picking date.

Process

Thermovinification or maceration, yeasts, temperature, oak.

Format and packaging

Bottle and its weight, closure, destination labelling.

Development

Samples until it is the wine

We prepare options against the agreed profile. You taste, correct, ask again. We adjust blend, ripeness, oak, sweetness, acidity. It does not get rushed.

A sample approved with doubts becomes a two-hundred-thousand-litre problem.

Validation

Before production, everything is frozen

The approved profile is documented: analytical parameters, composition, sensory description. That document is the technical contract for everything that follows.

It is what lets someone reproduce the same wine four years from now, with another harvest.

  • Analytical parameters

    The numbers that define the profile and against which every lot is approved.

  • Composition

    Varieties, proportions and origins of the approved blend.

  • Sensory description

    What the wine has to go on being when the harvest changes.

Production

From trial to scale

What works in a laboratory does not always work in a three-hundred-thousand-litre tank. Scaling is where badly conceived projects get lost.

We produce against the specification and verify every lot against the approved sample before it leaves.

Afterwards

A product ages too

The consumer who bought fourteen degrees now wants twelve. Heavy packaging becomes a regulatory problem. A competitor arrives with the same profile two dollars lower.

A product untouched for five years is not stable: it is ageing with nobody watching.
