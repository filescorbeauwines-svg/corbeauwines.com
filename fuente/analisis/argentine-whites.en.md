Argentina offers different ways to bring a white wine into a portfolio: from internationally recognised varieties through to propositions with an identity closely associated with the country. For a buyer, the opportunity begins by connecting that diversity to a specific need of their own customer.

A white for by-the-glass service, a food-led listing and a discovery proposition can call for very different wines. Understanding those differences makes it possible to request better samples and build an offer with a defined place in the market.

This piece is about what to settle before requesting the first sample, and how to read the available data without asking more of it than it says.

## Growth at different intensities

Argentine whites have been growing abroad, and the figure gets quoted often. It is worth opening up, because very different rates sit inside it.

These are the changes in export volume for the first half of 2026 against the same period in 2025, according to Argentina's Instituto Nacional de Vitivinicultura.

| Exports, by volume | January-June 2026 against 2025 |
| --- | --- |
| All white wines | +47.2% |
| White varietals | +5.5% |
| White varietals, packaged | +4.8% |
| White varietals, bulk | +9.2% |

FUENTE [INV, Comercialización de vinos y mostos. Mercado externo, June 2026, page 5. Provisional data](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/2018/10/comercializacion_mercado_externo_junio_2026.pdf)

> Growth reaches varietal whites as well, in both packaged and bulk, though at a very different rate from the headline total.

That gap between the total and the varietal figure matters when deciding. Statistics can distinguish businesses and rates of growth, but on their own they do not identify the commercial positioning of the final product: one statistical category can hold branded programmes, blends and supply operations working to different logics.

For a buyer weighing a white for their range, the practical conclusion is straightforward: the data describes a category in motion and documents concrete export activity, but demand for the product under consideration gets assessed in their channel and with their consumer. Current availability is confirmed with the supplier.

## What the offering is made of

If the decision moves forward, the next step is knowing the material available.

This is the composition of Argentine varietal white wine exports by volume in 2025, according to the INV report.

| Variety | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Chardonnay | 43.8% |
| Torrontés Riojano | 20.2% |
| Pinot Gris | 12.1% |
| Sauvignon Blanc | 12.0% |
| Other varieties | 11.9% |

PIE Share of varietal white wine export volume in 2025. Other varieties is derived by difference and may be affected by rounding. It does not indicate Argentina's share in any destination market.

Chardonnay accounts for four litres in every ten and allows entry under a name consumers already recognise. Torrontés Riojano brings an identity closely associated with Argentina. Pinot Gris and Sauvignon Blanc round out a diversity not always associated with Argentina.

Two cautions, so the chart is not over-read. A familiar variety does not prove it will sell through in a particular retail setting. And a cost-competitive origin does not on its own prove better value for money: that gets tested in the glass and on the shelf.

There is also a structural figure worth holding in mind. Argentina's white variety plantings fell 23% over the last decade and 1% in the last year, though white grape production recovered by 6% in 2026. Any programme needing sustained volume over several years should discuss that base before committing.

## The variety does not define the style

Settling the variety is only the beginning: the style still has to be agreed.

Asking for "an Argentine Sauvignon Blanc" does not define the wine. That name covers different aromatic intensities, different levels of perceived acidity, different textures, and choices about oak, sweetness and alcohol that entirely change the consumer's experience.

The same holds for Chardonnay, which precisely because it is a familiar name accommodates styles far apart from each other. And it holds in a particular way for Torrontés: its floral, intense character is frequently mistaken for sweetness, when the two are separate. A Torrontés can be aromatic and dry.

To assess samples with judgement, six dimensions are worth agreeing before tasting.

| Dimension | What to define before the sample |
| --- | --- |
| Fruit and aromatic expression | Which character is sought and how prominent it should be. |
| Freshness | What perceived acidity the wine needs for its drinking occasion. |
| Texture | Whether the aim is lightness and agility or more body and mouthfeel. |
| Sweetness | What perception of sweetness suits the product, decided separately from aroma. |
| Oak | Whether it features, how prominent it is and what job it does in the style. |
| Alcohol | What strength the destination market expects and how it affects the style. |

PIE A guide for the conversation between buyer and winery, not an official classification or a scoring scale.

With those six agreed, a tasting stops being a general impression and becomes a comparison between alternatives against explicit criteria.

## What job the bottle does

The step that organises everything above is deciding why the wine enters the range. Five possible jobs, offered as commercial hypotheses the buyer should test against their own channel.

- **White by the glass.** Prioritises approachability, freshness and consistency. Rotation matters more than singularity.
- **Food-led listing.** Needs enough texture and structure to accompany food, and usually performs better where staff can actively recommend the wine.
- **Discovery proposition.** Bets on a recognisable, unusual identity. Requires a channel able to explain it.
- **Sweeter profile.** Answers a defined audience. Demands clarity in communication so consumers seeking dry are not misled.
- **Style-led blend.** For when the experience sought matters more than the grape name.

None of the five is better than the others. What changes is what has to be asked of the wine, and therefore which sample is worth assessing.

## Where Argentine white already arrives

Destinations for Argentine white wine exported in 2025 include Brazil, at 16.9% of volume, the United States at 10.9%, the United Kingdom at 7.1% and Mexico at 4.6%. The full list includes other markets with intermediate shares.

FUENTE [INV, Special Report on White Varieties and White Wines, July 2026](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/informe-especial-variedades-blancas-y-vinos-blancos)

Those percentages describe how Argentine exports are distributed, not what position Argentina holds in each of those markets. Nor do they say anything about consumer taste in each country, which varies by channel, by region and by price segment.

What they do indicate is that a commercial path exists: there is logistics, there is labelling precedent and there is prior experience in those markets.

One example shows how closely variety, destination and format travel together. In 2025, of the 5,721.4 hectolitres of Argentine Pinot Gris shipped to the United Kingdom, 5,160 went in bulk: around 90%. The figure describes how that market is supplied with that variety, not final consumption or the kind of brand reaching the shelf.

## How supply is organised

Of total white wine exported in 2025, 79.6% left packaged and 20.4% in bulk. Worth clarifying that packaged does not mean bottle exclusively, and that these records do not distinguish between winery brands and private labels.

Choosing between a winery brand, a private label or bulk is a commercial and operational decision, distinct from selecting variety and style. It gets resolved on other criteria: brand control, investment, lead times, minimum volume and who does the bottling.

We develop those criteria in other Journal pieces: the economics of a private label, and how to compare bulk quotations.

What matters is that style, price, volume and format get defined together. The target price shapes the style available, the volume shapes the format, and the format shapes the cost again. Settling them separately usually means redoing the project.

## What to bring to the first conversation

An enquiry about whites goes much further when it arrives with these eight things defined.

- **Destination market.** Which country it sells in, since that shapes labelling, strength and style expectations.
- **Consumer and occasion.** Who it targets and in what situation the bottle gets opened.
- **Target shelf price.** The shelf price, not the wine price. Everything works backwards from there.
- **Style sought.** Described using the six dimensions above, or with a reference sample.
- **Sales channel.** Where it sells and whether staff can recommend it, since that changes which style works.
- **Supply continuity.** Whether the same profile is needed across successive campaigns or this is a one-off.
- **Volume and schedule.** For the first order and for the year, where annual requirements can be estimated.
- **Format.** Packaged or bulk, and who takes responsibility for the packaging.
